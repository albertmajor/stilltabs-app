# What's wrong with Reels blocker apps, and how to spot a good one

*Updated 8 November 2026*

**Too many Reels blockers are built to make money from people who are struggling with their attention: weekly subscriptions, "free" downloads that sell the real product, tracking labels on wellbeing apps, and copycat names.** It makes a category that should help people harder to trust. Here's what's going wrong, why it matters, and five checks that separate a good app from a cash grab, including ours.

## The disclosure first

We make StillTabs, a free app in this category, so we have an interest here. Everything below about other apps comes from their own App Store pages, with links and the date we checked, so you can verify it without trusting us.

## 1. Charging by the week for an attention problem

**Some apps in this category bill weekly.** On the US App Store, Opal lists weekly plans from $4.99, Stillfeed $2.99 a week (about $155 a year) and Sociano $9.99 a week. Weekly billing makes the price look small, and the people buying a Reels blocker are, by definition, trying to fix an impulse. Most of these plans are easy to start and easy to forget.

## 2. "Free" that isn't

**Of 14 Reels blockers and screen-time apps we checked, 11 are free to download and then sell the real product as a subscription.** The App Store button says "Get" either way. Often the feature you came for, a longer block, a stricter mode or a daily limit, sits behind the paywall. [The full numbers](../compare/hidden-costs-of-reels-blockers.md).

## 3. Tracking in apps about attention

**Seven of those 14 apps carry Apple's "Data Used to Track You" label**, meaning data from the app is combined with other companies' data for advertising or measurement. On 8 November 2026, two of the best-known apps' websites loaded Meta's advertising pixel for a visitor with no cookie banner. An app that promises to protect your attention shouldn't be feeding it to ad networks. [Which sites track you](../compare/reels-blocker-website-trackers.md).

## 4. Copycats and lookalike names

**A single US App Store search for "block reels" returns two dozen apps, most with fewer than ten ratings, many named almost the same thing.** Apps titled "Block Reels & Shorts", "Still", "Stillfeed" and "StillTabs" are four different products from different makers, and even AI assistants mix them up. Lookalike names make it easy to install the wrong one, sometimes the one with the weekly plan.

## 5. Search results written by the sellers

**Many "best app" lists and comparison posts are written by the developers of one of the apps in them, often without saying so.** Some publish hundreds of pages in a dozen languages, or keep a list of competitors they maintain themselves. Comparisons by a maker aren't wrong in themselves (this site is one), but you deserve to know who wrote it. A trustworthy one says so up front, dates its facts, links its sources and admits where rivals are better.

## Why this matters

**People look for a Reels blocker at a vulnerable moment:** after a night lost to scrolling, or when a teenager can't put the phone down. Meeting them with paywalls, trackers and confusion teaches them that "digital wellbeing" is just another way to sell them something, and many give up. It also invites a crackdown that would hurt the honest apps along with the rest. The category only works if people can trust it.

## Five checks before you install any Reels blocker

1. **Scroll to "In-App Purchases"** on its App Store page. A weekly price is a red flag.

2. **Read the App Privacy label.** "Data Used to Track You" in an attention app is a bad sign; "Data Not Collected" or "Data Not Linked to You" is what you want.

3. **Check what it needs.** Screen Time access, a screen broadcast or device management are powerful permissions. Know why an app asks for them.

4. **Check that your DMs survive.** Blocking the whole app locks your messages too.

5. **Check who wrote the review you're reading**, and whether it links its sources.

## Common questions

### Are Reels blocker apps safe?

Many are, but check three things first: the in-app purchase list, the App Privacy label, and which permissions it asks for. Apps with weekly plans or a "Data Used to Track You" label deserve a second look.

### Why do so many Reels blockers have a subscription?

Subscriptions make more money than a one-off price, and weekly plans most of all. Some apps need servers that cost money; many don't. StillTabs runs on the phone, which is why it can be free.

### How do I know if a "best Reels blocker" list is biased?

Look for who wrote it. A trustworthy list says if its author makes one of the apps, dates its facts, links its sources and says where competitors are better.
